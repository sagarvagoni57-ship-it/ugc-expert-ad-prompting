# Product facts, claims and audience utility

## Evidence ladder

Classify every product statement before writing it into dialogue or on-screen copy:

### Observed from the supplied image

Safe examples: bottle shape, label color, cap, visible size marking, fabric color, visible port, number of pieces, texture, button placement, box form. Say "the supplied image shows" internally; do not turn an unreadable detail into a fact.

### Source-supported

Use a first-party product page or reliable product documentation for intended use, dimensions, materials, compatibility and explicit feature wording. Attribute a seller claim as a claim, not as proven efficacy. Preserve the exact variant and market.

### Creative assumption

Location, wardrobe, time of day, creator casting, camera movement, hook and emotional direction are creative choices. Do not report them as product facts.

### Unknown

Rear packaging, hidden mechanism, ingredient list not provided, exact dose, shade number, price, stock, shipping, guarantee, clinical result, review count and availability. Omit or ask only if the unknown changes safe use or product identity.

## Claim rules for UGC dialogue

- The creator can point out what the label says, show an operation, compare visible options or explain a supplied use instruction.
- The creator cannot claim personal purchase, long-term use, a cure, a guaranteed transformation, a fake review, a fabricated customer quote or a result that the ad does not substantiate.
- Replace unsupported testimonial language with demonstration language:
  - Instead of "I used this for three months and it cured my acne," use "The bottle is labeled [verified product name]; follow the package directions and check the full ingredient list."
  - Instead of "This fits everyone," use "Here is the fit and the visible size/shape detail; check the size guide."
  - Instead of "This keeps you safe all day," use "The package states [verified protection detail]; follow its directions for application and reapplication."
  - Instead of "This will make your hair healthy," use "The tutorial shows the supplied method; the visible payoff here is smoother handling/appearance, not a guaranteed treatment result."

## Category-specific cautions

### Skincare, cosmetics and hair

Do not invent ingredients, SPF duration, shade number, universal match, acne/pore/anti-ageing result, treatment claim or application amount. Show label, texture, packaging and a safe, ordinary handling step. Avoid eye contact and dramatic before/after.

### Food and beverages

Do not invent flavour, nutrition, allergen status, health benefit or safe pet consumption. A tasting scene is a fictional creator action unless the user asks for an explicitly disclosed spec concept and the product information supports it. Keep portions and handling plausible.

### Supplements and wellness

Do not promise weight loss, cure, prevention, energy outcome or medical benefit. Use packaging and a neutral preparation/use demonstration, with the package directions as the authority.

### Fashion, shoes and accessories

Do not invent size accuracy, comfort, durability or performance. Show visible color, material, closure, styling, movement and a reminder to check the size guide when appropriate.

### Home and gadgets

Do not claim a capacity, battery life, compatibility, quietness, safety rating or durability that was not supplied. Demonstrate only the visible control or supported operation. Do not show destructive tests unless the product is expressly designed for them and the user provides evidence.

### Pets, children and baby products

Do not make safety, developmental or ingestion claims without authoritative product evidence. Never show an animal or child using a product in a dangerous, unsupervised or misleading way. A hands-only product demonstration is often safer.

## Audience utility check

Before writing the hook, answer three questions:

1. What situation is the US/UK viewer in right now?
2. What can they understand or decide from this 20-second demonstration?
3. What single action should they take next?

If the answer is only "the product looks cool," choose a more useful action: show how it opens, how it is styled, what the label says, how a control works, how the texture behaves, or what to check before buying.

## Disclosure and portfolio honesty

When the actor or voice is synthetic, or the work is a spec concept, keep disclosure separate from the spoken hook. Use an edit note such as `AI-generated fictional creator / spec concept / not a customer testimonial / not affiliated with the brand`, adapted to the platform and the client's legal guidance. Never present a fictional creator as a real customer or imply an endorsement that does not exist.
